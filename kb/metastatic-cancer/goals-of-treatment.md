# Goals of treatment

With metastatic cancer, the goal of treatment is often different from the goal earlier in
the illness. Earlier on, treatment may aim to cure the cancer. With cancer that has
spread widely, the aim is frequently to control it, to slow its growth, to ease symptoms,
and to help the person live as well as possible for as long as possible. This shift is not
a sign that the care team has given up. It is a change in what will help the most.

Treatments that may be offered include chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy,
hormone therapy, radiation, and surgery. Which ones are considered depends on the type of
the original cancer, where it has spread, and what the person wants. Many people move
through more than one kind of treatment over time as the situation changes.

Alongside treatment aimed at the cancer itself, there is palliative care, which focuses on
comfort. Palliative care is about managing pain, nausea, tiredness, breathlessness, and
the emotional weight of illness. It can be given at the same time as cancer treatment, and
starting it early does not mean stopping other care. It simply adds a team focused on
quality of life.

Decisions about treatment belong to the person with cancer and their care team. As a
caregiver, one of the most useful things you can do is help keep track of questions,
notice how your person is feeling day to day, and make sure their voice is heard at
appointments. You do not have to have the medical answers yourself.
