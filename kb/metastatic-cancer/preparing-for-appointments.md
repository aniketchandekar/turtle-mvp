# Preparing for appointments

Appointments can move quickly, and it is easy to leave one realizing you forgot to ask
something important. A little preparation helps you and your person get the most from the
time with the care team. You do not need medical knowledge to prepare well; you need your
questions and your observations.

Before the visit, it helps to jot down what has changed since last time. New or worse
symptoms, how well pain or nausea has been controlled, changes in appetite or sleep, and
anything that has been worrying either of you are all worth noting. Bringing a current list
of medications, or a photo of them, saves time and reduces mistakes.

Writing your questions down in advance is one of the most useful things you can do. Good
questions are often simple: What are we hoping this treatment will do? What side effects
should we watch for? Who do we call, and when, if something changes at home? What are the
options if this treatment stops working? It is completely reasonable to ask the team to
slow down or explain something again.

During the visit, taking notes or asking if you can record the conversation can help,
because it is hard to remember everything afterward. If you can, bring another person, or
join by phone, so there are two sets of ears. Afterward, a short summary of what was
decided and what to do next is worth keeping somewhere you can find it.
