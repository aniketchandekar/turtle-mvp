# What "metastatic" means

Metastatic cancer is cancer that has spread from the place where it first started to
another part of the body. The place where it began is called the primary cancer. When
cells break away from that primary tumor and travel through the blood or the lymph
system, they can start to grow in a new place. Those new growths are called metastases,
or metastatic tumors.

An important thing to understand is that metastatic cancer keeps the name of the place
it started. If breast cancer spreads to the lungs, it is called metastatic breast
cancer, not lung cancer. Under a microscope, the cells still look like breast cancer
cells. This matters because the type of the original cancer usually guides the kind of
treatment that is offered.

Doctors sometimes describe metastatic cancer as "stage 4" or "advanced" cancer. These
words can sound frightening, and it is completely normal to feel overwhelmed hearing
them. They describe how far the cancer has spread, not how much a person is loved or how
much time is left. Every situation is different, and questions about what a particular
stage means for your person are best answered by their own care team, who know the full
picture.

Common places for cancer to spread include the bones, the liver, the lungs, and the
brain, though it depends a great deal on the original cancer. Knowing where the cancer
has spread helps the care team explain what symptoms to watch for and what the goals of
care might be.
