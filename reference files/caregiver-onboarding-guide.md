# Caregiver Onboarding Guide — The First Conversation

**Principle:** Onboarding is a *phone conversation*, not a form. One ~15-minute call, caregiver first, patient second, everything else later. If it doesn't fit in 15 minutes, it waits — a crisis-time user will not fill out a 40-field intake.

---

## How the call flows

### Part 1 — About you (the caregiver) — ~5 min

> *"First, a few things about you — you're the one I'll be checking in with each week."*

| # | Question | Why we need it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your name, and how you're related to the person you're caring for | Identity + the family map starts here |
| 2 | Do you live with them, nearby, or far away? | Changes how we interpret what you can observe |
| 3 | Best phone number + best time for your weekly check-in call | The check-in is the heartbeat of the service |
| 4 | English or Spanish — and is it okay if we switch mid-call? | We'll speak whichever language *you* think in |
| 5 | *"If your mom couldn't make a medical decision tomorrow, who would make it — is that you?"* | This builds your family's decision-maker map — the single most important question we ask |
| 6 | One honest question: *how are you sleeping?* | We watch *your* wellbeing too, starting now |

### Part 2 — About the patient — ~8 min

> *"Now a few things about your mom — so that if you call at 2 a.m., I already know her situation and can get you to the right help fast. I'll only ask what changes how I help you."*

| # | Question | Why we need it |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Her name and rough age | Personalization |
| 8 | What kind of cancer, and where things stand — just diagnosed, in treatment, between treatments, finished? | Sets the care phase — our questions and alert thresholds change by phase |
| 9 | When was her last treatment, and what kind (chemo / radiation / surgery / daily pills)? | **The treatment clock.** Day 3 after chemo and day 18 after chemo mean different things for the same symptom |
| 10 | Her cancer clinic or oncologist — and *what number do they tell you to call after hours?* | **The escalation plan.** Every practice has one; we digitize the fridge magnet so 2 a.m. calls route correctly |
| 11 | Five quick baselines — over the past two weeks, on a normal day: pain? breathing? eating and drinking? alertness? any fevers or chills? | **The baseline.** Panic calls are about *changes* — we can only spot a change if we know the normal |
| 12 | Which one medication do you worry about most? | Start with one. On the next call, you can snap a photo of the pill bottles and we'll do the rest — no dictating long lists |

**Hospice families only:** hospice agency name + their 24/7 line. From then on, their nurse line is our first escalation path.

### Part 3 — Wrap-up — ~2 min

- Confirm the weekly call time ("Thursdays at 6, I'll call you")
- Explain the 24/7 number: *"Save this as 'Companion' in your phone. Any hour — I'll answer."*
- Reminder: anything we missed, we pick up next call. **No homework.**

## What the caregiver should know — privacy & data

The agent must say this plainly, early, and in human language — and it should be repeated in the first SMS summary in writing:

**1. You're talking to an AI.**
> *"I'm an AI companion, not a person. I can listen, coach, and connect you to humans — and any time you say 'I want a person,' I'll get you to one."*

**2. What we collect and why.**
- Everything you tell us goes into your family's private record: check-in answers, symptom notes, your wellbeing scores, documents you upload.
- **Purpose: three things only** — (a) answering your calls with your family's context, (b) spotting changes worth acting on, (c) getting the right human involved fast when needed.

**3. Who sees it.**
- **Your family circle** — only the people *you* add, and only what you authorize. The brother in Austin sees the weekly digest; he doesn't see your private burden scores unless you say so.
- **Your care team** — only if you ask us to share (e.g., the escalation summary when we connect you to the nurse line).
- **No one else.** We don't sell data. We don't use it for advertising. Ever.

**4. Consent for the patient's information.**
> *"Because I'll be holding details about your mom's health, I need to know she (or her legal decision-maker) is okay with that. Can you confirm she knows you're setting this up and is comfortable with it?"*
- If the patient has capacity: we note her verbal OK (and send a simple confirmation link she or the caregiver can complete).
- If she doesn't: the legal decision-maker authorizes. This is exactly why question #5 matters — it tells us whose permission counts.

**5. You're in control.**
- Correct anything, any time ("tap to fix" on every summary — corrections make the record *more* accurate).
- Delete anything, or everything — one request, full removal.
- Pause the weekly calls without canceling the 24/7 line.
- Export your family's record if you ever leave.

**6. What we will never do.**
- Diagnose, change medications, or interpret test results. We collect, coach, and connect — *clinicians decide*.
- Give legal advice. We explain, organize, and refer you to real attorneys and free legal programs.
- Guess about time remaining. Questions like "how long does she have?" go straight to the care team — with warmth, never with an answer we shouldn't give.

**7. Safety, honestly stated.**
> *"If you ever tell me about something that sounds dangerous — a fever during chemo, new confusion, trouble breathing — I won't just note it. I'll connect you to a human immediately, and I'll already have your mom's details ready for them."*

## Onboarding checklist (internal — what the call must capture before it ends)

- [ ] Caregiver identity + relationship + distance (local/remote)
- [ ] Language preference + weekly call slot
- [ ] **Authority seed:** who decides if the patient can't
- [ ] Patient consent path confirmed (verbal / link / decision-maker)
- [ ] Cancer type + care phase
- [ ] Last treatment date + type *(treatment clock anchored)*
- [ ] Clinic name + **after-hours number** *(escalation plan live)*
- [ ] Five baselines: pain / breathing / eating / alertness / fever-chills
- [ ] One worry-medication anchor
- [ ] AI disclosure delivered; privacy points 1–7 delivered
- [ ] TCPA consent for outbound AI-voice calls (recorded verbally)
- [ ] "Companion" number saved to their phone before hangup

**Deferred (captured on later calls or via the web page):** full med list (photo), insurance details, document uploads (PoA/advance directive), household map, preferred hospital confirmation.

---

## Design rules

1. **Caregiver first, patient second** — our user is the caregiver; patient questions exist to serve them.
2. **Every question earns its place** — it must change the next answer we give. If not, defer it.
3. **Conversation, not interrogation** — the agent explains *why* before sensitive asks ("…so I can get you to the right help fast").
4. **Exit ramps everywhere** — "we can come back to that" is always a valid answer; save-and-continue is native to a call.
5. **15 minutes, hard cap** — the onboarding metric is *% who complete their first weekly check-in*, not intake completeness.
